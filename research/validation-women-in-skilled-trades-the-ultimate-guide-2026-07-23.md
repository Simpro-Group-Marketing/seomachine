## Strict Source Proof

- Public labor-market claims use BLS or DOL primary sources. Third-party vendor articles are excluded as factual proof.
- Each occupation profile must preserve the distinction between May 2025 BLS annual mean wage and OOH median wage. Only the annual mean figures in this sidecar may be called `average annual pay`.
- National averages do not predict individual earnings. Location, experience, licensing, union coverage, specialization, employer, schedule, and overtime can affect actual pay.
- Outlook percentages describe projected employment change from 2024 to 2034. They are not a guarantee of individual job availability.
- Public work-context statements must remain within the mapped DOL-sponsored O*NET detailed profile. BLS OOH pages remain research provenance for training-duration and advancement review. Where the claim-ready artifact says advancement evidence is unavailable, the rewrite may pose a due-diligence question but must not attribute a formal path to BLS or O*NET.
- State and local licensing requirements vary. No universal license, certification, training duration, or eligibility promise is approved.
- The resolved BLS series page supplies occupation counts. CPS Table 11 separately publishes a 4.3% women figure, but public copy uses the counts and does not calculate a new share.
- DOL's FY 2023 active-apprentice figure and its 2021 construction-and-extraction figure describe different populations and years. They must not be merged into one trend.
- No exact quote, named reviewer, customer metric, rating, ranking, badge, testimonial, or competitor claim is approved.
