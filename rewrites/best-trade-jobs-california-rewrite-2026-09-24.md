# 18 Highest-Paying Trades in California to Consider in 2026

The highest-paying trades in California are elevator installation, power-line work and substation repair, based on 2025 median pay. [Elevator and escalator installers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/47-4021.00?st=CA) lead at $141,180, and [power-line installers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/49-9051.00?st=CA) follow at $129,040. [Substation repairers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/49-2095.00?st=CA) earn $121,520, [mechanical insulation workers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/47-2132.00?st=CA) $119,690 and [building inspectors](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/47-4011.00?st=CA) $101,290. Every figure is a California statewide median from the federal Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics survey.

If you're comparing trades that pay well in California, salary is a smart first filter. Pay is only half the picture, though. The list below covers hands-on construction, installation, maintenance and repair trades. Each entry adds the path in, the credentials worth planning around, the levers that raise pay and the day-to-day work.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Elevator, power-line and substation trades top the California pay list, and each one starts without a four-year degree.
> - Electrical, plumbing and HVAC work sit right outside the top 18 on median pay, yet their highest earners clear six figures.
> - Most trades on this list begin after high school with paid on-the-job training or a registered apprenticeship.
> - Licensing, specialization, union coverage and overtime explain much of the pay gap inside each trade.
> - The long-term ceiling for licensed tradespeople is running a contracting business of their own.

## 18 highest-paying trade jobs in California

Power, utility and building-systems trades hold the top of the list, and aviation and heavy equipment trades fill the middle. Each profile covers California pay, the work itself, the path in, what raises earnings and what to know before you commit. Where a trade needs a state or federal credential, the link goes to the issuing agency.

### 1. Elevator and escalator installers and repairers

- **California salary:**
  Elevator installers earn a median of [$141,180 in California](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/47-4021.00?st=CA), and the top 10% make $173,920 or more.
- **What they do:**
  Elevator mechanics assemble, test and repair elevators, escalators, moving walkways and wheelchair lifts in offices, hospitals, transit stations and apartment towers.
- **How to become one:**
  Union and employer apprenticeships are the standard start after high school. California adds a [Cal/OSHA certification for conveyance mechanics](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dosh/elevatorcertification.html), earned through an approved apprenticeship or a qualifying exam.
- **Why earnings rise:**
  Modernization work on older buildings keeps demand steady, and emergency callouts for stuck cars add premium hours.
- **Good to know:**
  Shafts, machine rooms and pits are the workplace. Expect tight spaces, heights and heavy components.

### 2. Electrical power-line installers and repairers

- **California salary:**
  Lineworkers earn a median of [$129,040 in California](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/49-9051.00?st=CA), and the top 10% make $167,090 or more.
- **What they do:**
  Lineworkers build and repair the overhead and underground lines that carry power from plants and substations to homes and businesses.
- **How to become one:**
  Utilities and line contractors train new workers through long-term on-the-job programs and union apprenticeships that open with climbing, rigging and electrical theory.
- **Why earnings rise:**
  Storm restoration and wildfire recovery bring long shifts and overtime, and journey-level status raises the base rate.
- **Good to know:**
  Heights, weather and high voltage come with the job. For a wider view of workforce demand, see these [electrical industry statistics](https://www.simprogroup.com/blog/electrical-industry-statistics-2026).

### 3. Powerhouse, substation and relay electrical repairers

- **California salary:**
  Substation and relay technicians earn a median of [$121,520 in California](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/49-2095.00?st=CA), and the top 10% make $165,540 or more.
- **What they do:**
  Transformers, breakers and protective relays inside generating stations and substations need regular inspection, testing and repair, and this trade handles all three.
- **How to become one:**
  A postsecondary electrical or electronics certificate is the common starting point, followed by utility training on relay testing and switching.
- **Why earnings rise:**
  Protection and relay skills take years to build, so utilities pay a premium for technicians who hold them.
- **Good to know:**
  Shift work and on-call rotations are part of the role because substations run around the clock.

### 4. Mechanical insulation workers

- **California salary:**
  Mechanical insulators earn a median of [$119,690 in California](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/47-2132.00?st=CA), and the top 10% make $165,540 or more.
- **What they do:**
  Insulators wrap pipes, ducts, boilers and tanks in industrial plants, hospitals and commercial buildings to control heat, cold and sound.
- **How to become one:**
  Union apprenticeships are the main door in. California's [Division of Apprenticeship Standards](https://www.dir.ca.gov/das/DAS_overview.html) oversees the registered programs that end in journeyperson status.
- **Why earnings rise:**
  Refinery and industrial projects anchor the top of the pay scale, and firestopping skills add another line of work.
- **Good to know:**
  California employs a small number of these workers, so read the pay estimate as a rough guide.

### 5. Construction and building inspectors

- **California salary:**
  Building inspectors earn a median of [$101,290 in California](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/47-4011.00?st=CA), and the top 10% make $161,550 or more.
- **What they do:**
  Foundations, framing and electrical, plumbing and mechanical work all pass an inspector's review against building codes before a project moves forward.
- **How to become one:**
  Inspection is a second-career trade. BLS lists five or more years of related work experience as the entry route. Electricians, plumbers and carpenters make the move later.
- **Why earnings rise:**
  Certifications across multiple disciplines open larger commercial projects and plan review work.
- **Good to know:**
  Site walks, reports and plan reviews replace most of the physical labor.

### 6. Control and valve installers and repairers

- **California salary:**
  Control and valve technicians earn a median of [$100,260 in California](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/49-9012.00?st=CA), and the top 10% make $137,030 or more.
- **What they do:**
  Meters, valves, regulators and thermostatic controls on gas, water and industrial systems are the core equipment they install and fix.
- **How to become one:**
  After high school, moderate on-the-job training gets new workers started, and utilities run their own training programs.
- **Why earnings rise:**
  Gas distribution and industrial process work sit at the high end, and troubleshooting skill opens senior technician roles.
- **Good to know:**
  Field routes mean driving between sites and working in trenches, vaults and plant rooms.

### 7. Telecommunications line installers and repairers

### 8. Avionics technicians

### 9. Operating engineers and construction equipment operators

  Prevailing-wage public projects and GPS grade-control work pay at the top of the range.

### 10. Commercial and industrial electrical and electronics repairers

### 11. Aircraft mechanics and service technicians

## What affects trade salaries in California?

Trade pay in California rises with licensing level, specialization, union coverage, overtime and the region where you work. Compare each median with the figure in parentheses to see how far skill and experience move earnings inside one trade. The levers below explain most of that spread. You control a few of them.

- **License and journeyperson level:** Apprentices earn a share of the journeyperson rate that climbs as they log hours. California's [Division of Apprenticeship Standards](https://www.dir.ca.gov/das/DAS_overview.html) registers the programs that set those steps.
- **Union coverage:** Union members in construction and extraction jobs earned a national median of [$1,561 per week in 2025](https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/LUU0254815900). Nonunion workers in the same jobs earned [$1,004 per week](https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/LUU0254829700).
- **Overtime and shift work:** Storm response, plant shutdowns and night paving add premium hours. Under California [overtime law](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_overtime.htm), nonexempt workers earn one and one-half times their regular rate for hours over eight in a workday.
- **Specialization:** Niche skills such as relay testing, medical gas and fiber splicing mark the top earners in their trades.
- **Service and maintenance work:** Maintenance agreements and repeat service calls keep HVAC, electrical and alarm technicians busy year-round, which steadies income between projects.
- **Region:** Metro pay differs across California, and each wage link in the table breaks pay down by metro area, so check yours before you move.
- **Business ownership:** A licensed tradesperson who runs a contracting business earns the margin on each job in addition to a wage.

## How to choose the best trade job in California for you

Pay matters, but the right trade also fits your body, schedule and long-term plans. Work through the questions below before you commit to an apprenticeship or training program. Your answers narrow the table to two or three trades worth a closer look. They also give you sharper questions for people who already work in them.

1. How long a training route suits you, and does it pay while you learn?
2. Which credential or license does the trade need, and how soon do you earn it?
3. How do you feel about heights, confined spaces, weather and heavy lifting?
4. Do you prefer steady service routes or project-based construction work?
5. Is the trade growing, and does it open a path to estimator, inspector or owner?
6. Would you rather skip a four-year degree and its student debt?

## Frequently asked questions

### What trade gets paid the most in California?

Elevator and escalator installation is the highest-paid trade in California, with median pay of [$141,180 in 2025 state wage data](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/47-4021.00?st=CA). The top 10% of these workers make $173,920 or more. Power-line installers and substation repairers follow, and none of the three calls for a four-year degree.

### What trades can make $100,000 a year in California?

Five trades pay a California median above $100,000: [elevator installers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/47-4021.00?st=CA) at $141,180, [power-line installers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/49-9051.00?st=CA), [substation repairers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/49-2095.00?st=CA), [mechanical insulation workers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/47-2132.00?st=CA) and [building inspectors](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/47-4011.00?st=CA) at $101,290. Top earners clear that mark in other trades too, and [California electricians](https://www.onetonline.org/link/localwages/47-2111.00?st=CA) in the highest-paid tenth make $140,340 or more.

### What are the best trade jobs in California without a four-year degree?

The best trades in California for pay all start without a bachelor's degree. [BLS entry data](https://data.bls.gov/projections/occupationProj) lists a high school diploma or a postsecondary certificate for each one, followed by on-the-job training or an apprenticeship. Elevator, power-line and substation work pay the most among these routes, and apprentices earn wages while they train.

### How long does it take to learn a trade in California?

Plan on roughly 18 months to four years or longer. The [FAA mechanic certificate](https://www.faa.gov/mechanics/become) calls for 18 months of practical experience for one rating. [Cal/OSHA elevator mechanic certification](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dosh/elevatorcertification.html) calls for three years of conveyance industry work. A license from the [Contractors State License Board](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/contractors/applicants/contractors_license/exam_application/experience_for_exam.aspx) takes four years of journey-level experience.

### Do union trade jobs pay more in California?

Yes, union trade workers earn more on average. Nationally, union members in construction and extraction jobs earned a median of [$1,561 per week in 2025](https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/LUU0254815900). Nonunion workers in the same jobs earned [$1,004 per week](https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/LUU0254829700). In California, union apprenticeships also set pay steps that rise as apprentices log hours, which makes the premium visible from the start.

### Are trade jobs in demand in California?

Yes, and demand runs strongest in energy and building service trades. [BLS projections](https://data.bls.gov/projections/occupationProj) show 2025 to 2035 national growth for HVAC technicians at 10.9 percent, power-line installers at 10.3 percent and electricians at 9.2 percent. Solar installers lead at 36.5 percent, while telecom line installer jobs shrink by 3.4 percent.
