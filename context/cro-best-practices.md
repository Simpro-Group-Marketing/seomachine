## Above-the-Fold Checks

Visitors should understand four things quickly:

1. What the offer is.
2. Who it is for.
3. Why it matters.
4. What to do next.

Use a clear headline, a short supporting line, one primary CTA, and a visible trust or clarity signal only when that signal has approved evidence.

## Headline Mechanics

Good CRO headlines are specific, concrete, and easy to scan. Common patterns:

- Outcome plus constraint: “Get [outcome] without [pain].”
- Problem plus next step: “Stop [problem]. Start [better behavior].”
- Use case plus audience: “[Workflow] for [audience].”
- Question plus payoff: “Ready to [outcome]?”

Avoid vague superlatives, broad category clichés, and claims that cannot be sourced.

## CTA Mechanics

Primary CTAs should be:

- Action-led.
- Short enough to scan.
- Matched to the conversion goal.
- Visually distinct from secondary actions.
- Repeated at natural decision points on longer pages.

CTA specificity belongs in nearby helper copy when the button itself would become too long.

## Form Mechanics

Ask only for fields needed to route, qualify, or fulfill the offer. For every field, document why it is needed, where the data goes, and whether the conversion value justifies the friction. Use progressive disclosure when extra detail is helpful but not essential.

## Trust and Proof Mechanics

Trust signals should be close to the claim or action they support. Use:

- Logos only when usage is approved.
- Metrics only when source-bound.
- Quotes only when exact wording is approved.
- Review themes only when the workflow’s review policy permits paraphrase.

This file does not provide those approvals.

## Page-Flow Checklist

- One primary conversion goal.
- Clear above-the-fold value proposition.
- Logical proof or objection handling before high-friction CTAs.
- Short sections with scannable headings.
- Mobile tap targets of at least 44 by 44 pixels.
- No unnecessary exit paths on paid-traffic landing pages unless required for compliance, privacy, or consent.
- No carousel or hidden critical proof.
